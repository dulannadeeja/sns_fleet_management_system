Voice and tone

How we write, everywhere: website, WhatsApp, emails, ads and the trip page. Our voice never changes. Our tone moves with the moment, from excited on the home page to calm and serious when something goes wrong.

Why us, not a big tour company

Our core message. Big companies run the same fixed round tour for every guest, with scripted guides, no stops beyond the plan and commission stops. We are a young local crew who stop when something is worth it, never shop for commission, and plan real Sri Lankan experiences as Sri Lankans.

"Not another big-coach round tour."

The home page section headline. Under it: "Most Sri Lanka tours run the same fixed route for every guest, stop where the commission is, and drive past everything else. We plan it the way we travel our own island."

How to say it

Compare practices, never people or names.

Rules

  1. Say "a typical big tour", never a company's name. Naming and attacking a company can break comparative-advertising rules in Europe, and it sounds bitter.
  2. Describe what happens (fixed route, commission stops, scripts), not insults ("boring", "lazy", "scam").
  3. Every "with us" line must be true on every trip. If a driver ever takes guests shopping, this message breaks.
  4. No "without limits": extra stops are free within about 15% more driving, and daily driving limits apply. Say "Extra stops are part of the trip".
  5. Prove it with real moments: our own photos of family lunches, hidden waterfalls and nights out.
  6. Tone stays warm and confident, never angry. We are excited about our island, not against anyone.

Tone by moment

The same voice, turned up or down. Energy and playfulness drop as money, time pressure or problems rise.

Writing rules

  1. Short sentences, one idea each, about 20 words at most. Guests read English as a second language.
  2. Common words. If a travel word is needed (tuk-tuk, hoppers), explain it once in a few words.
  3. Moment first, place second: "Spot a leopard at sunrise", then "Yala National Park".
  4. Be exact: times, kilometres, hours and prices from the system, never typed by hand. Example prices always say "sample".
  5. Sentence case everywhere. No ALL CAPS. At most one exclamation mark on a screen, and only Kotiya uses it.
  6. Buttons start with a verb and say what happens: "Book with a 30% deposit", not "Submit".
  7. Only claims we can prove today. Licence numbers only once issued. Reviews only real, with their source.
  8. No fake urgency: no "only 2 left", no countdowns, no "limited offer".
  9. Contractions are fine (we're, you'll). They sound like a person.
  10. Formats: USD 1,910 · 8:47 am · Tuesday 24 November · 7 nights · 12 h.

Small text

Buttons, errors, empty screens and confirmations carry the most weight per word.

Languages

English and German

English first, German at launch too (twin pages). German is written by a person, not machine-translated, and keeps the same short, warm voice. German runs about 30% longer, so keep English tight.

OpenEnglish spelling: British or American? My recommendation: British (colour, centre), because most guests are European.

OpenGerman: "du" or "Sie"? My recommendation: "du", like GetYourGuide and Airbnb in German, because it fits a young, friendly crew. "Sie" feels safer for older guests.

Sinhala

Only short greetings and thanks, always next to the English, never alone.

Before anything goes out

  • Would a guest with basic English understand it on a phone, in a tuk-tuk?
  • Is every number, price and time from the system, and are samples marked?
  • Is every claim true today?
  • Is the tone right for the moment (no jokes near money or problems)?
  • Does every button say what happens next?
  • Round trips: no "free cancellation". Day trips: "Free cancellation until 24 hours before".
  • Is the German version ready, and does it still fit the layout?
